# Scopa theme: research notes

What the theme is drawn from. The Neapolitan pattern (*carte napoletane*) is the
deck Scopa is usually played with in southern Italy.

## The deck
- 40 cards, four suits: **Denari** (coins), **Coppe** (cups), **Spade** (swords),
  **Bastoni** (clubs/batons). Ranks Asso (1) to 7, then three court cards:
  **Fante** (8), **Cavallo** (9), **Re** (10).
- Spanish-type suit signs: clubs as "logs or cudgels", swords "shorter and
  straight", cups smaller, coins drawn as star/sun-burst discs.
- Court cards are **full-length, single-ended** figures standing on coloured
  platforms. No double-ended versions exist. Hair and moustache styles are
  16th century.
- Cards are plain white with **no frame and no indices**. The suit sign floats
  in a top corner on the court cards. Standard size is 83 × 50 mm.
- **Asso di Denari:** an uncrowned double-headed eagle holding the great
  ring, with a tax-stamp circle below.
- **Settebello:** the 7 of Denari, the most valuable single card in Scopa.
  Its coins are laid out 2-1-2-2.
- **Cavallo di Spade:** a Moor-like rider in a turban with a scimitar.
  **Re di Denari:** sometimes called *matta* (wild card in sette e mezzo).
  **3 di Bastoni:** the "Gatto Mammone" grotesque mask.

From the reference card scans (Dal Negro), which the figures are drawn after:
- **Fante di Coppe:** green beret with red feather, grey-blue bob, green and red
  doublet with blue placket, gold puffed breeches, red garter bows, green
  hose. Raises a gold chalice in one hand and holds a gold stick in the other.
  Stands on a red platform.
- **Cavallo di Denari:** grey horse walking left; the rider wears a gold turban,
  red sleeves and a green jerkin, and holds a coin high. Gold platform drawn
  as outlined boxes.
- **Re di Denari:** gold crown, white hair and beard, blue doublet with gold
  sleeves, green breeches, gold hose, blue cape lined red, a long sword in
  hand, coin floating at top right. Gold platform.
- **Asso di Coppe:** a great lidded urn with a red shoulder, blue gadroons, a
  gold belly with a mask, a green base and white handles.
- **Coin pip:** an ink-ringed white band, then a gold disc with a sun-burst and
  a small core. **Cup pip:** a gold chalice with a knop and a red foot.

## Backs and case
- Backs vary by maker. Modiano's is a "signature 3D checkered pattern"; Dal
  Negro packs come in red cases, and Modiano sells red and blue versions. The
  theme uses blue tumbling-block ("3D") checks inside a white border, plus a
  red tuck case with a gold double frame and a coin medallion. These are
  shapes only, with no maker marks or text.

## Scopa itself
- *Scopa* means "broom": taking every card on the table "sweeps" it and scores
  a point. Four cards are dealt face up to start. Settebello, *primiera* and
  most coins also score. In the theme a broom sweeps the table hand clean
  now and then, and the cards are dealt again.

## Gaps
- No reference scans of the Spade and Bastoni court cards could be fetched
  (Wikimedia rate-limited), so those figures aren't used. The sword in the
  Re di Denari's hand follows the "short and straight" description.
